### Gameplay / movement
- **[visual] Stepping out to pass is sideways, not diagonal** (user, 2026-11-18, stage 12, 0.6.0-dev.2, three screenshots; drift switch on). A passing Gunner walks straight sideways into the passing lane, but returns home diagonally (looks natural). Cause: during a lane change a mech counts as being in both lanes (`shareLane`: from and to), so the friendly it is passing, right ahead in the lane it is leaving, keeps blocking its forward step (gap rule) until the change is done; forward speed 0 → perpendicular. On the way back the home lane ahead is free, so it can drift.
  - **A. Block only while still overlapping sideways (proposal).** During a lane change, a friendly in the lane being left blocks only while the two still overlap in x (|dx| < radii sum + small margin). It steps out sideways a little, then goes diagonal: a short hook, then the same look as the return. Physically consistent, small change, applies to every lane change (passes, fan-outs).
  - **B. Ignore the friendly being passed from the start.** Fully diagonal, but the two sprites overlap for a moment at the start.
  - **C. Leave it.**
  - **Decided (user, 2026-11-18): A. Built in 0.6.0-dev.3** (no switch; `STEP_OUT` 4 px clearance). Test scene: passing a stopped Mortar, the Gunner steps out about half a lane, then goes diagonal (gains ~50 px during the change instead of 5).
- **[gameplay] Support (Aegis) should never lead** (user, 2026-11-18; not decided). The Aegis is fragile and weak in a fight, yet it walks into certain death when nothing is in front of it: deployed alone, or when the friendly it followed dies. Today (role Support): it stays `follow` (40) behind the frontmost *fighting* friendly in its own and the neighbouring lanes, and behind marching friendlies in its lane; with nobody ahead it simply walks on. The enemy AI never deploys an Aegis alone (it comes last in a group), so this is mainly the player's Aegis.
  - **A. Hold unless escorted.** It only advances while a friendly non-Support mech is ahead of it within an escort distance (e.g. 300) in its own or a neighbouring lane; otherwise it stops where it is (still shoots and shields). Simple; also covers "the friendly ahead died": it stops instead of walking on.
  - **B. Wait for a passer.** Deployed with nobody ahead, it stands at the deploy point (base or forward turret) until a friendly passes it, then follows that one. Same as A in practice for a lone deploy; A's rule is the general one, B describes how it looks.
  - **C. Fall back.** When nobody is ahead any more, it walks back toward its base until a friendly is ahead again or it is behind its own turrets. Safest, but a new behaviour (walking backwards), more code, and it can leave the friendlies behind it without the shield.
  - **D. Leave movement, make it sturdier** (more HP). Doesn't fix walking alone into the enemy.
  - **Proposal: A** (which gives B's look for a lone deploy): never the frontmost mech; stop when the escort is gone or too far ahead. Same rule for the enemy's Aegis. Open: the escort distance; whether it should count a friendly on a strip as escort (proposal: yes).
  - **User (2026-11-18):** tends to A, but sometimes deploys an Aegis far behind the troops so it catches up to support them; with a fixed escort distance it would just stop. Both are valid designs.
  - **A2 (proposal, replaces A): "never the first one the enemy meets".** It advances while a friendly non-Support mech is ahead in its own or a neighbouring lane (any distance, strip friendlies count) **and** that friendly is closer than the nearest enemy ahead in those lanes (mech, turret or base). It still never passes the frontmost friendly (today's `follow` distance). Catching up works: troops far ahead, nothing between → it walks up to them. Safe: an enemy between it and the troops, or nobody ahead → it stops (and shoots/shields from there). It starts again as soon as a friendly passes it or the way is clear. No distance to tune.
  - **On hold (user, 2026-11-18):** today's rules sound fine on reading them in full; the user observes the Aegis more in play first, then we decide whether to change them a little (A2 stays the candidate).

    - *The Brawler doesn't hit enemies in the next lane.* Intended until the side attack (step 3).

    - Scout and Breacher change lanes to charge correctly.

Fixes Mortar.f blocking Brawler-Gunner and the Brawler behind a Seeker.f. Support keeps "never passes a fighting friendly".

- **[balance] Kill bounty helps the winning side snowball** (user, 2026-11-14)
  - **Current rule:** the player gets 25% of the destroyed unit's cost for every kill, mechs and turrets alike. The enemy AI gets no bounty (it has its own income, which grows when your mechs push close to its base).
  - **Problem:** whoever is ahead gets richer and pulls further ahead. But energy for killing *attackers* near your own base is good: it helps a defender recover.
  - **Options:**
    - **A, no bounty for turrets** (user idea): destroying turrets no longer feeds an attack that's already winning.
    - **B, lower the bounty overall,** e.g. 25% → 15%: simple, but it weakens comebacks too.
    - **C, bounty by location:** 25% for kills in your own half of the field (defending), 10% in the enemy half (attacking). This matches the example "my base is under attack, I kill attackers, I get a boost".
    - **D, comeback scaling:** the bounty grows when your base has less HP left than the enemy's. Very targeted, but harder to see and understand while playing.
  - **Recommendation: A + C:** no bounty for turrets, 25% for mech kills in your own half, 10% in the enemy half. Easy to explain and it rewards defending, not snowballing.
  - **Open point:** should the enemy AI get the same defensive bounty, so both sides follow one rule? Today it gets none.
